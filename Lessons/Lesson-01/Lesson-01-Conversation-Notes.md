## 2. French-to-English Phrasing Polish (12 Upgrades from Today)

| # | Focus Point | ✗ Direct Translation | ✓ Natural Native English | 💡 Why / Rule |
| :- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Teach vs. Learn** | *"I tried to learn them a language"* / *"She does not want to learn it to him."* | **"I try to teach them a language."** / **"She doesn't want to teach it to him."** | In French, *apprendre* means both. In English, the learner **learns** and the parent/teacher **teaches**. |
| **2** | **Upbringing vs. Education** | *"Children in the same house receive the same education."* | **"Children in the same house have the same upbringing."** | *Education* = school/university. **Upbringing** = how children are raised at home. |
| **3** | **Explain + TO** | *"I'm always explaining people..."* | **"I always explain to people..."** | Always include **to** before the person with *explain* (*explain to someone*). |
| **4** | **Frequency** | *"One in a week, I go to solo swing lessons."* | **"Once a week, I go to solo swing lessons."** | Say **once a week** (1x), **twice a week** (2x), **three times a week** (3x). |
| **5** | **Hard ON oneself** | *"He is very hard with himself."* | **"He is very hard on himself / self-critical."** | Collocation: **to be hard on someone**. |
| **6** | **Indirect Question Order** | *"Try to understand how works things."* | **"Try to understand how things work."** | Keep Subject + Verb order inside embedded clauses (*how things work*). |
| **7** | **Taking feelings on board** | *"We listen and take it in consideration."* | **"We take their feelings on board / into consideration."** | *Take [something] on board* is a very natural British & international phrase. |
| **8** | **Leading Meetings** | *"Today I started to talk because nobody took it in charge."* | **"Today I stepped up because nobody was taking charge."** | Say **take charge** (without *it in*) or **step up**. |
| **9** | **Campsite vs. Camping** | *"Going to a camping for the holidays."* | **"Going to a campsite (or going camping)."** | *Camping* = the activity. **A campsite** = the physical place. |
| **10** | **Duration** | *"It's been one month that there is not a lot of rain."* | **"We haven't had much rain for about a month."** | Use Present Perfect + **for** to express ongoing duration. |
| **11** | **Riding a Bike** | *"It's like a bicycle... you warm up and it come back."* | **"It's just like riding a bike—once you warm up, it comes right back."** | English includes the action verb: *like **riding** a bike*. |
| **12** | **Often** | *"We do not see them oftenly."* | **"We don't see them very often."** | *Often* (/ˈɒf.ən/) is already an adverb (never *oftenly*, and the *t* is silent!). |
