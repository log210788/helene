# How-To-Teach Guide: Lesson 02
## Unlocking Visual Fluency & Spontaneous Description with Helene

This guide provides real-time pedagogical tactics, psychological cues, and correction scripts for Lewis when teaching Lesson 02 using the authentic Monument Valley lightning photograph.

---

## 1. Teacher Mindset & Coaching Nuances for Helene

### 🧠 Helene’s Specific Friction Points
1. **The "Word-Search Freeze":** When Helene encounters a specialized landscape term (e.g. *butte*, *crepuscular rays*, *sandstone formation*), her instinct may be to pause and mentally translate from French or reach for Google Translate.
   - **Tutor Rule:** Do NOT give her the word immediately. Give her permission to describe its function or appearance using the **3-Way Circumlocution Formula** (*"It looks like...", "It reminds me of...", "It's situated right between..."*).
2. **French Phonetic Defaulting:** Under cognitive load, French speakers default to equal syllable timing (monotone stress) and drop the initial /h/.
   - **Tutor Rule:** Never interrupt her mid-thought to correct pronunciation. Note it in the chat window, and drill it during the targeted Pronunciation Clinic (Phase 3).
3. **From Conversational Ease to Executive Precision:** In Lesson 01, Helene proved she is an engaging conversationalist. Lesson 02 pushes her to add precision adjectives (*brooding, stark, arid, dramatic, electrifying*) instead of relying on generic words like *nice, big, bad, beautiful*.

## 2. Minute-by-Minute Teacher Delivery Scripts

### Opening & Transition to the Image
> *"Helene, remember in our last session how we talked about the unusually dry weather in eastern France, and you mentioned the hosepipe bans? Well, take a look at today's photo—nature decided to bring all the rain and electricity in one single second!"*

### How to Guide the Visual Exploration (Without Lecturing)

| Stage | What Helene Might Say | Teacher Coaching Prompt |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1: Overall Impression** | *"It's very big rock with dark sky and lightning."* | *"Spot on! How does the mood feel? If you were standing right there on that dirt road, what sounds or sensations would you experience?"* |
| **Stage 2: Spatial Layers** | *"There is one rock here, and another rock behind."* | *"Great spatial eye! In English, we call these rocky towers 'sandstone buttes' (/bjuːts/). Let's divide the image: What do you see in the foreground, in the middle, and up on the horizon?"* |
| **Stage 3: The Light & Lightning** | *"The light is coming through the clouds on this rock."* | *"Yes! Those are sunbeams or shafts of light. Notice how the lightning bolt hits right behind the right-hand butte. What kind of contrast does that create?"* |

## 3. Real-Time Error Correction & Recast Protocols

### The "Silent Recast" (Keep Conversational Momentum)
When Helene makes a minor grammar slip, repeat her sentence back naturally with the correct form without halting her flow:
- **Helene:** *"The lightning is falling on the floor."*
- **Lewis:** *"Yes! The lightning is striking the ground right behind the butte."*
- **Helene:** *"We do not see this oftenly in France."*
- **Lewis:** *"Exactly, we don't see this very often at all."*

### The "Pronunciation Lightbulb" (Phase 3)
When practicing the key vocabulary:
1. **The Aspirated 'H' (Horizon & Harsh):**
   - *"Helene, put your hand in front of your mouth. Say 'horizon'. You should feel a puff of warm air on your palm: /hɒˈraɪ.zən/. Try that three times with me."*
2. **Stress-Timing Punch:**
   - *"In French, each syllable has equal weight: mo-nu-ment. In English, we punch the first syllable and squash the rest: **MON**-yuh-mənt. Say it like a drum beat: **MON**-yuh-mənt."*

## 4. Bridging to the Workplace Simulation (The Incident Pivot)

### Why This Matters for an Automotive ERP Analyst
In Helene’s day job, unexpected bugs or data synchronization failures occur without warning. Describing a sudden event with clarity, calm authority, and structured language is the hallmark of a C1 communicator.

### The Setup Script:
> *"Helene, let's look at this lightning bolt as a metaphor. In automotive software, when a critical EDI catalog feed or warehouse inventory sync crashes out of nowhere, it feels just like a lightning strike. In your next standup, how would you describe a sudden critical bug to an international team? Let's practice using: 'Out of the blue...', 'The system was hit by...', and 'We're currently investigating the root cause...'"*
