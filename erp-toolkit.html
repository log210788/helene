💨 The "H" Warm-Air Rule

Feel warm air on your palm when pronouncing the /h/ sound. Never add an "H" to vowel-start acronyms!

✅ Breathed /h/ "We have a hybrid cloud setup with hosted clusters."
⚠️ Zero "H" (Vowel Starts) "An ERP system" • "An application" • "An error"

⚡ The 3 Past-Tense `-ed` Rules

Sound When to Use Tech Examples
/ɪd/ (+1 Syl) Ends in T or D test-ed, up-dat-ed
/t/ (No Syl) Unvoiced (k, p, s, sh, ch) checked, developed
/d/ (No Syl) Voiced vowels, m, n, l, r, v deployed, configured

🛡️ Professional Time-Buyers (Kill the "Euhhh...")

Instead of falling silent or translating from French, launch straight into one of these time-buying anchors:

"Essentially, what happens is..." Gives you 2 seconds to structure the technical explanation.
"What I mean to say is..." Perfect for self-correcting or clarifying a complex workflow.
"Under the hood, ..." Signals to your colleagues that you are moving from high-level to code/database specifics.
"In a nutshell, ..." Ideal for summarizing status updates or concluding your turn in a standup.

💡 The 3-Way Circumlocution Formula

Forgot the exact English technical word? Don't reach for Google Translate. Describe it using:

1. Function ("What it does") "It's the specific script / API that syncs the inventory levels..."
2. Analogy ("What it's like") "It works pretty much like a bridge between the warehouse scanners and our ERP core..."
3. Impact ("Why it matters") "The main takeaway is that it prevents duplicate part orders from being submitted."
